Welcome to your shipyard.

Did that new weapon, console, or trait actually improve your ship? Let's find out.

A combat log is the starting point. A better build is the goal.

Record your current setup, collect a few runs, change one thing, and compare the results. STO Shakedown shows where damage came from and how performance varied, so you can decide what to test next.

Your first comparison · quick checklist

Download instructions ↓
  1. Record: type /combatlog 1 in STO, play a run, then select your GameClient log folder here.
  2. Create a profile: type a ship name for reference, then create its loadout and Baseline variation with equipment notes. Profiles are not linked to ships in STO.
  3. Analyze and save: select logs recorded while flying that ship and setup. Choose the correct combat stretch and character, review the results, and save to the matching variation with the patrol, difficulty, and Solo/Group.
  4. Repeat: collect several comparable baseline runs. Change one item, create a new version, and save several new runs.
  5. Compare: choose both versions, the same character, and the same patrol, difficulty, and Solo/Group. Check averages, ranges, damage sources, and incoming damage/healing.

Keep your build unchanged within each saved run. Match difficulty and team conditions. “Entire log” includes idle gaps and may include several missions. Three runs per version is a starting point, not proof.

Create your ship profile

Type a ship name for your reference, then create a loadout and Baseline variation with equipment notes. The profile is not linked to your ship in STO; you assign the matching combat runs yourself.

03

Analyze and save a run

Select the logs recorded while flying that ship and loadout. Choose the correct combat stretch and character, review damage and survivability, then save to the matching variation with the patrol conditions.

04

Change something. Test again.

Create another version describing the change. Save new runs, then compare the same player, ship, mission, and difficulty. Look at damage contributions and variation across runs.

Example: did a new beam weapon help Milwaukee?

Baseline: beam array setup → save three comparable runs.
Change: replace one array with a Hyper-Dual DBB → create a new version and save three more runs.
Compare: did overall damage improve, or did one weapon gain while the rest of the ship lost output?

The app shows measured differences. It cannot yet determine the cause or automatically recommend equipment. Match test conditions and change one thing at a time.

01 Select your combat data

Choose logs recorded while flying the ship and loadout you want to evaluate. Your selected ship profile does not automatically match or filter these files. If you changed ships within a log, choose only the matching combat stretch. Select one log, or hold Ctrl and select adjacent rotated files to combine a split fight. Combined imports are sorted by event time (up to 12 files / 128 MB). Check the resulting combat selection before saving.

What am I selecting? An encounter is a stretch of combat with all players and enemies recorded during it—not a duel against the enemy named in the list. We split stretches after 60 seconds without a logged event. The target names are recognition clues, not filters.

Choose an individual encounter or “Entire log” to include everything in this file. Then select the player whose ship you want to inspect.

How this prototype measures a run

Encounters are separated by a 60-second gap between log events. Long pauses may split a fight; back-to-back fights may merge. Check the time and targets before saving.

DPS is hull plus shield damage divided by the entire detected encounter duration, with a one-second minimum. It may differ from parsers that use player-active time.

Build equipment and encounter difficulty are entered by you. The app does not infer firing arcs, button presses, or why a change caused a result. Damage taken and logged outgoing healing are descriptive metrics, not survivability scores.

No cloud uploads or AI calls. Settings, build notes, and saved summaries are stored in the app’s data folder.
